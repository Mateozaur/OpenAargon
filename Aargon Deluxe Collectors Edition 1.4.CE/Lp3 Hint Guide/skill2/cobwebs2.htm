Clue: "The differences make the difference"

This clue is not so hard to figure out. Firstly, that which "makes the difference" is normally an action or thing which alters a situation in such a way as to make something work, or make the seemingly impossible possible and so on. In our case that just means "to win the level". So this is a play on words where the word "difference" has two distinct meanings within the same sentence. The second "difference" we already know, but what about the first one? Well it turns out that the first use of the word refers to differences in color. Looking at the level and the laser sources we see 3 different colors reappearing:

A) in the selectors
B) in the laser sources
C) in the coins

We also have some white coins to deal with. Well if there were no white coins the level would be trivial: We'd simply send each beam through the middle, hitting coins corresponding to the beam's color. Unfortunately life isn't always that easy. The first thing we should realize is that the one thing we definitely cannot do is send the beams in a direction which hits coins corresponding to that beam's color. Why? Well, just imagine you did just that with say, the yellow beam. Yellow contains red and green, and the remaining 4 colored coins are magenta and cyan. To make magenta we need blue and red. To make cyan we need blue and green. We need to spin the whites as well so we can't do the same thing we did with yellow for sure. Also we'll need to send some blue into the yellow beam for it's white coin. But we need two blues to make our colors so we can't afford to throw one away. So yellow can't align with yellow coins. Using the same logic neither beam can align with coins corresponding to it's color. So there's one "difference" we need to obey: Beams must start out in a direction different from the direction of the line through colored coins of a the beam's color. So that means we'll have to use the selectors to select-out unwanted colors from our beams and then insert them somewhere else. So that means that the color of the selectors must be different from the colors of the beams that use them. So another difference must be obeyed! Well it turns out there is exactly one way to satisfy these two conditions simultaneously (incidentally solving the puzzle). Hence the required differences force the solution thereby "making the difference"!

Special note:
Did you notice that this "clue" spells out in precise detail exactly how to solve the puzzle? That's right, this so-called "clue" is actually the puzzle's solution! What's more, this explicit spelling-out of the solution requires exactly one noun! The remaining words are "the" and "make", which simply modify said noun (ie. "difference").
